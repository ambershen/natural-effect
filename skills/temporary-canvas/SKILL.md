# Temporary Canvas

Begin with the creative action: leaving a message, revealing an image, retracing a memory, or drawing together. Choose a material whose response gives that action meaning. This recipe grew from imagining a foggy window as a temporary canvas and extending the experiment to sand and snow.

## Compose a material recipe

Specify the input action, changed material state, visible response, sound if any, and behavior after release. A reusable recipe includes these decisions and their adjustable ranges, not just a background texture.

| Surface | Contact changes | After release | Sound direction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Condensation | Fingertip clears a feathered path; broad wiping reveals more of the scene | Moisture gradually clouds the view; droplets are optional and version-specific | Silence is valid; rubbing is an optional experiment |
| Sand | Contact displaces grains into furrows and rims | Steep edges settle; marks persist until redistributed or covered | Quiet grain motion follows speed and contact area |
| Snow | Dwell compacts powder; movement pushes some material to the rim | Edges settle; fresh snow can gradually cover impressions | Define and audition separately; do not assume an accepted audio recipe |

Gravity acts through local redistribution or falling deposits. It does not mean moving an entire drawn word down the screen. Fog recovery can occur without visible drops; preserve the chosen study's no-droplet preference. Never apply a shared global fade to all materials merely to make marks temporary.

## Keep expression adjustable

Expose only controls that serve the piece: contact width, resistance, accumulation, persistence, and replenishment. Name them perceptually unless there is a measured physical model. Keep separate mappings for touch, pen pressure, and camera gestures. Ordinary touch and camera movement cannot establish real hand force.

Start with a single material and compare the same slow stroke, swipe, hold, and release across variants. Combine behaviors only after the individual responses are readable. For example, snowfall covering a message is a proposed storytelling variation; distinguish it from a tested installation.

## Package and verify

Keep a small recipe record: intent, input mapping, material rules, tunable values with units, sound provenance, accepted demo, rejected alternative, and untested devices. Include source modules when available; this skill itself supplies guidance rather than an executable renderer.

Verify both drawing and the full life of the trace after release. Check whether texture, movement, and audio tell the same material story. Avoid declaring an effect natural from a screenshot or build alone.

Optional companions: `simulate-natural-fog`, `simulate-natural-sand`, `simulate-natural-snow`, and `natural-motion-interaction`. Each remains independently usable; use the table above if companions are unavailable.
